Campaign Amplification Hackathon 
Core Challenge: Build a polished, functional mobile prototype that helps people create, review, fund, and amplify messages so they can reach audiences beyond their existing networks.
Use the reference website to understand the product concept and journey. The attached task brief provides the detailed requirements. Your job is to translate the workflow into an excellent mobile-first experience; not to reproduce a desktop website screen-for-screen.
Task Overview
The Campaign Amplification Platform is a community-funded advertising and message-amplification product. Individuals, creators, nonprofits, organizations, businesses, and community groups can create campaigns around messages they want more people to see. The platform reviews eligible campaigns, backers pool contributions toward distribution costs, and approved funds are used to promote the message to audiences beyond the creator’s existing network.
This is not a donation platform. A contribution supports the distribution and reach of a campaign message; it does not directly fund the cause, person, or organization described in that message.
The goal of this hackathon is to build a functional mobile application prototype that demonstrates the core product journey:
Discovering campaigns
Understanding campaign stories and supporting sources
Creating and submitting campaigns
Contributing toward campaign promotion
Tracking funding and distribution progress
Viewing campaign reports
This is not about building a production-scale advertising platform in a short time.
It is about clarity of product thinking, clean UI/UX, strong backend logic, and smooth execution.
The interface should feel simple; the underlying workflow should remain complete.
 What You Need to Build
Create a working mobile application prototype for the Campaign Amplification Platform that covers the core flows described below.
Your prototype should feel like one connected product—not a landing-page mockup or a collection of disconnected screens.
Scope Rules (Important)
A mobile application prototype is required. Use React Native with Expo.
UI/UX quality is a critical part of the challenge. Visual polish alone is not enough: navigation, information hierarchy, readability, feedback, and clarity of the next action matter just as much.
Preserve the product’s core concept: users contribute toward promoting a message, not donating directly to the cause.
The key journey should work end-to-end, including campaign creation, draft saving, review status, campaign discovery, contribution flow, and progress tracking.
Use a five-step campaign creation flow in the specified order: Basics → Story → Campaign & Ad Visuals → Reach → Submit.
Do not insert unnecessary setup screens between these five steps.
Allow users to browse campaigns without creating an account.
Applicants may add thoughtful features if they improve trust, usability, accessibility, or the core product experience.
Additional features should not distract from completing the primary journey.
Use the provided website and attached task document as references while making deliberate mobile-first design decisions.

 User Roles
1. Audience
People who discover a campaign, read its story, inspect its sources, understand the intended audience, share it, and provide feedback.
They must be able to browse campaigns without creating an account.
2. Campaign Creator
An individual, influencer, nonprofit, organization, business, or community group that wants to distribute a message to a wider audience.
Creators can:
Build campaigns manually
Use AI-assisted drafting
Describe their campaign through a voice-based creation experience
Edit campaign drafts
Submit campaigns for review
Monitor funding
View distribution reports
3. Backer
A person who contributes toward the cost of promoting a campaign.
Before contributing, backers should understand:
The campaign message and story
Supporting sources
How their contribution will be used
Estimated audience reach
Applicable fees
Campaign funding progress
Public backer names should be private by default, while payment records remain accurate and compliant.
4. Platform Administrator / Reviewer 
An internal role that reviews campaign content and sources, checks creative assets and targeting, approves eligible campaigns, prepares distribution, manages campaign states, and monitors reporting and moderation.
This role is essential because approved funds are managed for message distribution rather than simply transferred to creators.
One account may act as an audience member, a campaign creator, and a backer.

 Campaign Lifecycle
Implement a clear, state-driven workflow:
Draft → Submitted for Review → Changes Requested or Approved → Available for Funding → Funding Goal Reached or Funding Period Ends → Distribution Preparation → Promoting → Completed
Support exceptional states such as Rejected, Cancelled, Refund Pending, and Failed where applicable.
Funding completion must not automatically imply that advertising has launched.
Distribution begins only when required approval, payment, budget, and advertising setup conditions are met.
 Design & UX Guidelines
UI/UX is one of the most important evaluation areas.
The product contains unfamiliar concepts—campaign funding, advertising distribution, estimated reach, review, and use of funds—so the interface must make them understandable without overwhelming people.
The application should feel premium, calm, credible, readable, and easy to use.
Use:
Mobile-first layouts designed for real phone screens and one-handed use
A consistent spacing scale
Clean typography and restrained brand colors
Consistent iconography, card styles, and interaction patterns
Strong visual hierarchy so users understand each screen and its next action
Rounded campaign cards and high-quality campaign imagery
Clear progress indicators
Expandable information sections
Contextual explanations of unfamiliar concepts
Smooth but purposeful transitions and subtle feedback
Accessible contrast, readable text, and sufficiently large touch targets
Clear form labels and validation messages
Skeleton loading states and useful error messages
Appropriate loading, empty, error, and success states
Clear confirmation after saving, submitting, backing, or changing campaign status
Different Screens Require Different Priorities
Campaign Story Screens: Prioritize editorial readability, context, and visible sources.
Backing Screens: Prioritize trust, use of funds, estimated reach, fees, and the contribution action.
Creator Screens: Prioritize progress, saved work, validation, and the next required step.
Do not fill every screen with charts, badges, cards, or decorative elements. Keep secondary details expandable and make the next action obvious.
Avoid dead ends, inconsistent back behavior, unclear icons, and buttons that appear functional but do nothing.
Make demo data visually credible and consistent across feeds, detail pages, funding progress, and dashboards.
UX Test: A first-time user should be able to explain what the platform does, what their contribution pays for, and what happens next without needing a team member to guide them.

 Points to Note
UI/UX: Clean, intuitive, trustworthy, and user-friendly design is critical. The experience should be easy to understand even for people unfamiliar with community-funded advertising.
Product Thinking: Preserve the distinction between contributing to message amplification and donating directly to a cause. Explain funding, fees, estimated reach, review, and distribution clearly.
Code Quality: Use a well-structured project, reusable components, sensible naming, and maintainable code.
End-to-End Functionality: Campaign creation, saving, preview, submission, review, discovery, contribution, and progress tracking should work as one journey.
State Handling: Include loading, empty, error, success, pending, and validation states for major flows.
Security & Privacy: Validate data on the server, protect secrets, enforce role permissions, and avoid exposing private backer or payment details.
Use of AI Tools: You may use Cursor or similar AI tools to improve productivity. Use them thoughtfully, review generated code, and take ownership of the result.
